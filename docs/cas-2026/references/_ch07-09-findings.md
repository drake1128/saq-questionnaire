## Notable Findings

### Chapter 7 — TCAR, CAS vs CEA Decision Making

- **TCAR adoption accelerating in the USA**: The VQI-Medicare database analysis (Zarrintan 2023, n=51,803) showed TCAR and CEA have similar 1-year mortality regardless of symptomatic status, but TCAR had slightly higher 3-year death in symptomatic patients (HR 1.33). This suggests TCAR is best suited to asymptomatic patients or those with high anatomic/surgical risk. **2022 lecture content citing only early ROADSTER data is now outdated**; ROADSTER 2 one-year results (Kashyap 2022) confirm 0% stroke rate in 155 high-risk patients.
- **ESVS 2023 guideline (133 recommendations)** now rates CAS as equivalent to CEA for standard-risk asymptomatic patients in experienced centres (Class IIa, Level A), directly endorsing TCAR as a preferred option over transfemoral CAS for patients at high surgical risk (Class IIb). This is a significant upgrade from the 2017 ESVS position.
- **ESC 2024 PAD Guidelines** (Mazzolai et al.) align with ESVS 2023 on CAS/CEA equivalence in asymptomatic patients managed in experienced centres, and include new sections on polyvascular disease management — relevant for decision-making in complex CAS candidates.
- **SVS 2022 guideline** (AbuRahma et al.) remains the key North American reference, recommending CEA as first-line for symptomatic patients (50–99%) at low surgical risk and for asymptomatic patients ≥70% stenosis with <3% institutional stroke/death rate; CEA preferred over transfemoral CAS for low-risk patients. **Gap**: SVS 2022 does not strongly endorse TCAR (insufficient evidence at time of publication); subsequent VQI and ROADSTER data now fill this gap.
- **2024 lecture content** on TCAR indications should be updated to reflect: (1) TCAR now approved for standard-risk patients in the USA since 2022, (2) ESC/ESVS 2023-2024 guideline convergence, (3) TOPGUARD study testing next-gen TCAR with CGuard micromesh stent.

### Chapter 8 — Surgical Approaches / CEA

- **ACST-2 (Lancet 2021)** is the definitive landmark trial (n=3,625 asymptomatic patients, mean 5 years follow-up) showing CAS and CEA have equivalent risk of disabling or fatal stroke (1% procedural risk each; ~0.5%/year thereafter). CAS had marginally higher non-disabling procedural stroke (2.7% vs 1.6%); CEA had more MI and cranial nerve palsy. **Lectures citing only CREST or earlier trials are now superseded by ACST-2's superior sample size**.
- **Wang et al. 2022 meta-analysis (Stroke)** pooled 7 RCTs (n=7,230 asymptomatic patients) confirming: CAS and CEA have comparable composite perioperative outcomes (OR 1.13), but CAS carries higher perioperative any-stroke risk (OR 1.62, p=0.004) and non-disabling stroke risk (OR 1.81). No significant long-term difference in composite stroke/death/MI.
- **Modified eversion CEA meta-analysis (Lee 2026)** — the most recent evidence (6 retrospective cohorts) shows meCEA associated with significantly lower postoperative stroke vs conventional CEA (p=0.01), and reduced shunt/patch use. No RCT data yet; this is a promising technique not yet covered in 2022/2024 lecture slides.
- **Cost-effectiveness (Akkara 2025)**: CEA marginally more cost-effective than CAS (ratio 1.019) at 1 year but difference is small ($21,264 vs $21,433). CAS offers shorter procedure time (77 vs 192 min) and shorter LOS (2.6 vs 3.1 days). **Lecture content citing older cost models needs updating**.
- **2022/2024 lecture gap**: eversion CEA (eCEA) vs conventional CEA comparison and new patch closure vs primary closure evidence should be incorporated; the 2023 ESVS guideline provides updated guidance on shunt use, patch type, and timing.

### Chapter 9 — Difficult CAS Tips and Tricks

- **CIRSE 2024 Standards of Practice** (Spiliopoulos et al.) now categorically lists **type III aortic arch and extensive calcification as absolute contraindications to transfemoral CAS** — the preferred alternative being transradial/brachial (TR/TB) with proximal protection or TCAR. This is the most authoritative technical guidance document available (2024). **2022 lecture content on arch classification as a "relative" challenge is outdated**.
- **Transradial access for CAS** — the pooled analysis (Batista 2024, n=1,166) showed 95% procedural success, 6% crossover rate to transfemoral, 2% ARAO, and 2% CVA within 30 days. The meta-analysis by Du et al. 2024 (Angiology, n=1,300) confirmed slightly lower success rate vs femoral but clinically acceptable and with fewer access site complications.
- **TR/TB with proximal protection (Mo.MA Ultra)** — Montorsi et al. 2022 (556 patients, 38% left bovine arch, 67% radial, 33% brachial) demonstrated procedural success in all patients (ITT), with the novel "No.MA2" technique (no-mandrel 2-wire) enabling difficult-anatomy procedures that would otherwise require femoral fallback. **No coverage of this technique in 2022 lecture content**.
- **Simmons guiding catheter via transradial** (Muszynski/Gory 2023, PMC12280257) — 7F Simmons catheter achieved 89.3% success in symptomatic patients, with only 7 (9.3%) crossovers; **provides a practical roadmap for type III arch and bovine arch via radial approach** — specific technique not covered in 2022/2024 lectures.
- **New device innovation**: TOPGUARD study (Trystula 2024) tests next-generation TCAR using CGuard MicroNET-covered stent with flow reversal — early results promising for calcified/complex plaque. **Dual-layer stents** (as detailed in CIRSE 2024) reduce plaque protrusion but may have slightly higher early thrombosis rates — requires DAPT beyond 6 months. Neither is covered in existing lecture slides.
- **Key 2024 update for Ch9**: Direct carotid access (surgical cutdown) and superficial temporal artery access (Myrcha 2024, Int Angiol) remain valid salvage options for patients unsuitable for both CEA and standard CAS. This should be added to "salvage maneuvers" section.

## Sections of 2022/2024 Lectures Most Likely Outdated

1. **TCAR indications**: Original lectures pre-date standard-risk TCAR approval (2022) and ESVS 2023 Class IIa endorsement.
2. **CEA vs CAS for asymptomatic patients**: Lectures citing ACT-1 and early CREST only; ACST-2 (2021) is the definitive update.
3. **Unfavorable arch anatomy as absolute contraindication to CAS**: CIRSE 2024 now formalises type III arch/extensive calcification as hard stop — lectures should redirect to TR/TB or TCAR.
4. **Eversion CEA technique**: 2026 meCEA meta-analysis not available for 2022/2024 lectures; technique evidence now supports meCEA in experienced hands.
5. **Radial access**: Systematically absent from pre-2023 CAS lectures; now supported by multiple series and meta-analyses (2023–2024).
