### Notable Findings — Chapter 1

- **Intersocietal Accreditation Commission (IAC) 2023 revised duplex criteria**: Ponukumati & Stableford (2026, Semin Vasc Surg) highlight that the IAC revised its consensus velocity criteria in 2023, revising the post-stent surveillance thresholds and updating PSV cut-offs. The widely used 2003 Society of Radiologists in Ultrasound (SRU) criteria (ICA PSV <125 cm/s = normal; PSV >230 + EDV >100 = ≥70%) remain the most commonly cited, but face criticism for variability. **Slides from 2022/2024 may use pre-2023 IAC criteria.**
- **Post-stent restenosis surveillance**: Updated velocity criteria for post-stent surveillance differ significantly from native artery criteria; PSV >300–350 cm/s and ICA/CCA ratio >4.0 used for ≥50% in-stent restenosis. This is not well reflected in many older chapter slides.
- **Plaque vulnerability — IPH is the key biomarker**: Bos et al. (JACC 2021, Rotterdam Study, n=1349) confirmed intraplaque hemorrhage (IPH) on MRI is independently associated with incident stroke (HR 2.42, 95% CI 1.30–4.50). This validates extending carotid US assessment beyond stenosis degree to plaque morphology with echolucency as a proxy.
- **Standardization gap**: The Mukabagorora 2025 scoping review confirms major global variation in duplex protocols with no universally adopted standard; the 2003 SRU criteria dominate but are criticized. Course slides should acknowledge this controversy.
- **Contrast-enhanced ultrasound (CEUS)**: Not covered by any major guideline in 2021–2026 as a standalone recommendation, but emerging evidence for intraplaque neovascularization remains a research area.

### Notable Findings — Chapter 2

- **ESCR 2023 twin-consensus documents** are the current standard for CT/MR carotid plaque imaging protocols. They formally endorse moving beyond stenosis degree toward multiparametric plaque assessment: IPH, lipid-rich necrotic core (LRNC), fibrous cap status, ulceration, and positive remodeling. **This represents a paradigm shift not captured in pre-2023 course slides.**
- **ESCR PSV/EDV table** (reproduced in Part I): ICA PSV ≥230 + EDV ≥100 + PSV ratio ≥4.0 = ≥70% stenosis; near-occlusion = variable velocities. This aligns with 2003 SRU but the ESCR consensus uses it as baseline only before CT/MR.
- **Intracranial VW-MRI (vessel wall MRI)**: Chen et al. Stroke 2024 review from Columbia University confirms CTA/MRA remain lumen-based first-line tools for ICAS; high-resolution VW-MRI adds value by differentiating atherosclerosis from vasculitis, dissection, and reversible cerebral vasoconstriction syndrome. Currently mainly a research tool.
- **VW-MRI biomarkers predict stroke recurrence**: Zhang et al. meta-analysis (Eur Radiol 2025, 18 studies, n=2240) found plaque enhancement (OR 2.18), T1W hyperintensity (OR 2.58), and plaque thickness (SMD 0.27) significantly predict recurrent ischemic stroke. **This is actionable evidence for VW-MRI in clinical ICAS workup that is not reflected in 2022/2024 chapter slides.**
- **DSA gold-standard comparison**: WASID and SAMMPRIS established DSA-based 70–99% stenosis as high-risk threshold; the Gutierrez Lancet Neurol 2022 review confirms CTA and MRA have sensitivity/specificity of ~90% vs DSA for ≥50% stenosis; DSA still needed for interventional planning.
- **FMD/dissection imaging**: Not addressed by a specific 2021–2026 guideline; best covered in ESC 2017/2021 PAD guidelines.

### Notable Findings — Chapter 3

- **ACST-2 (Lancet 2021) — major landmark trial**: n=3625 asymptomatic patients; 5-year follow-up (2008–2020); CAS vs CEA resulted in similar 5-year non-procedural fatal/disabling stroke rates (2.5% vs 2.5%); total stroke slightly favored CEA (4.5% vs 5.3%, p=0.33, NS). **Conclusion: CAS and CEA are comparably safe in expert hands for asymptomatic patients. This is foundational evidence for chapter content.**
- **CMS policy change October 2023**: US Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services expanded CAS coverage to include standard-risk symptomatic (≥50%) and asymptomatic (≥70%) patients. This has caused a significant shift in practice patterns (CAS utilization increased 28%/year since 2017; post-2023 shift further accelerated). **Chapter content written before October 2023 will not reflect this.**
- **SVS 2022 guideline key points**: CEA recommended over CAS for symptomatic low-risk patients with 50–99% stenosis; CEA or CAS acceptable for asymptomatic 70–99% if perioperative risk <3%; carotid intervention recommended within 14 days of stable stroke onset (not 6 weeks as previously).
- **CMOSS trial (JAMA 2023)**: EC-IC bypass for symptomatic ICA/MCA occlusion with hemodynamic insufficiency did NOT show significant benefit over medical therapy alone (primary outcome 8.6% bypass vs 12.3% medical, p=0.39). **This definitively does not support EC-IC bypass for carotid occlusion in most patients.** Chapter content advocating bypass should be qualified.
- **Near-occlusion management**: CIRSE 2024 guideline acknowledges near-occlusion (string sign) as a distinct entity with variable velocity patterns; revascularization is technically challenging. Recent multicenter data (Pagliariccio 2024, Ann Vasc Surg) supports CEA for near-occlusion without full luminal collapse, but not for full collapse (diameter <2mm).
- **ESC Council Consensus 2025 (Musialek, Cardiovasc Res)**: Introduces the "Neuro-Vascular Team" concept (analogous to Heart Team); advocates individualized, precision medicine approach integrating imaging biomarkers (IPH, plaque echolucency, microemboli on TCD). Recommends revascularization for high-risk asymptomatic subgroups beyond stenosis alone.
